# Recorded Project Presentation Instructions

## Overview

Instead of holding an in-person presentation class on **Wednesday, December 2**, you will record and submit a short video presentation showcasing **one** of your projects from this semester. Doing this asynchronously gives you space to present your work thoughtfully (and walk away with a portfolio-ready demo), while allowing everyone to engage more deeply with each other's projects through peer review.

- **Choose one project:** Project 1 (*Telling a Narrative*), Project 2 (*Supporting Decisions*), or Project 3 (*Inviting Participation*).
- **Target length:** 5-7 minutes
- **Due date:** Wednesday, December 2 at 4:45 PM
- **How to submit:** Upload your video along with a link to your live GitHub Pages URL using the form shared in Slack. Your live app on GH Pages does not need to be entirely complete, but should be functional enough to demonstrate your project's core features and user interactions.

## What Your Presentation Should Focus On

This is **not** a line-by-line code walkthrough or a button-by-button feature tour. While you should briefly touch on key technical or data choices, **the heart of your presentation should be the user and your design thinking**: *why* this project needed to exist, *who* it is for, *how* you designed it to serve them, and *how* you arrived at your final solution.

Use the following structure to organize your presentation:

### 1. Justify the Need ("Why does this project exist?")

- What problem, question, decision, or community gap motivated this project?
- Why does this issue matter, and why is an interactive web map / geospatial interface the right way to address it?

### 2. Dive Deep into Your Guiding Statements

Revisit the guiding statements from your chosen project and unpack them thoroughly. Go beyond one-sentence answers to explain who your users are, what context they operate in, and what they actually need:

- **If presenting Project 1 (Telling a Narrative):**
  - *The story I want to tell is...* / *The question(s) I want to answer is(/are)...*
  - Who is your target audience, and why should this spatial pattern matter to them?

- **If presenting Project 2 (Supporting Decisions):**
  - *The users I want to empower are...*
  - *The specific decisions those users need to make are...*
  - *The data required to inform these decisions is...*

- **If presenting Project 3 (Inviting Participation):**
  - *The specific participants/community I want to engage are...*
  - *The contributions/input I am inviting them to make are...*
  - *The participants will benefit from contributing their input by...*
  - *The users that will use the collected data are...* *(same as or different from the participants)*
  - *The collected data will benefit those users by...* *(if different from the participants)*

### 3. Explain Your Thought Process ("How did you get here?")

- Walk us through how the project evolved from your initial idea and wireframes to the finished application.
- Share key design decisions and trade-offs: What did you intentionally include, and just as importantly, what did you cut, simplify, or change along the way?
- How did working with the data or putting yourself in your user's shoes reshape your original plan?

### 4. Walk Through the Live App Through Your User's Eyes

- Demo your deployed application as if you are your intended user (or guiding them through a realistic scenario).
- Explicitly connect interface choices (map symbology, narrative pacing, filters and linked charts, or contribution and feedback flows) back to the goals in your guiding statements.

### 5. Technical & Data Reflection

- Briefly highlight 1-2 technical or data decisions that meaningfully shaped the user experience (e.g., how you structured or simplified data, handled persistence, approached interface responsiveness, or navigated a data limitation/bias).
- Avoid reading code aloud unless a brief snippet directly explains a user-facing challenge. Keep it grounded in the user experience.

### 6. Honest Reflection & What's Next

- If you had two more weeks, or were preparing to put this in front of real users, what is one thing you would refine, test, or rethink?

## Practical Tips for Recording

- **Screen + voiceover** is all you need (webcam optional). An easy way to record is using Zoom, but feel free to use whatever tool you prefer.
- **Make it readable:** Zoom your browser in slightly if needed so text, legends, and charts are easy to read on a laptop screen.
- **Do one quick dry run:** Check your audio levels and timing so you stay within 5-7 minutes. Clarity and honesty about your process matter far more than polished video editing.
