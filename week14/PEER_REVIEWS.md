# Constructive Peer Review Instructions

## Overview

After presentations are submitted, you will be assigned **three** classmates' projects to watch and critically review.

- **Assignments posted:** Thursday, December 3 via Slack
- **Reviews due:** End of the semester (December 17, 11:59 PM)
- **Where to submit:** Add your review to the pull request associated with the project you are reviewing.

## Your Role as a Reviewer

You are **not** grading your classmates on code completion, hunting for minor bugs, or judging technical complexity for its own sake.

Instead, your job is to act as a **thoughtful design and product critic**: evaluate how well each project's solution fits its stated user needs and justifications, highlight what the project does well, and identify concrete opportunities for improvement.

---

## How to Conduct Each Review

1. **Watch the full presentation** carefully, paying attention to the presenter's target users, guiding statements, and design rationale.
2. **Explore the live app (briefly):** Open their deployed link for 2-3 minutes and try using it from the perspective of the user they described.
3. **Write a structured, _brief_ review** covering the four sections below (you need not go longer than 300-500 words per project, unless you really have more to say; be substantive, specific, and respectful of the presenter's effort).

### 1. Alignment: Solution vs. Use Case Justification

- How compellingly did the presenter justify the need for this project?
- Looking at their guiding statements (target audience, decisions supported, or community participation model), how well does the actual application serve those goals?
- Where is the fit strongest? Are there any gaps between what the tool *aims* to do for its users and what the interface currently enables, or elements that don't seem necessary for the stated use case?

### 2. What the Project Does Well

- Call out **2-3 specific strengths** in the design, framing, data communication, or user experience.
- Explain _why_ each choice works for the intended user (e.g., _"Syncing the map zoom with each narrative beat made the neighborhood disparity immediately clear"_ rather than _"Nice map!"_).

### 3. Opportunities for Improvement

- Identify **2-3 concrete, actionable opportunities** to make the project more effective for its target users.
- Try to anchor your suggestions in the user's experience and stated goals, not personal preference or code nitpicks. For example:
  - _Instead of:_ "You should add more charts."
  - _Try:_ "Since a planner needs to prioritize where to intervene first, a ranked summary list or clear threshold indicator alongside the map would make that decision faster."
  - _Or:_ "The contribution form asks for six fields; making the optional ones collapsible could lower friction for residents on a phone."

### 4. One Question or Next Step to Consider

- Close with one thoughtful question about their design trade-offs, data assumptions, or how they might test or extend this with real users.

---

## Guidelines for Constructive Feedback

- **Be specific:** Reference concrete moments in the video or specific interactions in the app.
- **Evaluate against _their_ goals:** Critique how well the project serves _their_ stated users and use case ahead of how you would have built a different project.
- **Balance encouragement with candor:** Generic praise doesn't help someone grow, and harsh criticism shuts colleagues down. Aim for the kind of honest, generous feedback you would want before putting a project in your portfolio.
